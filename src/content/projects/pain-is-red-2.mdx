Pain is red.
Red like a goat’s bleats battling for breath,
Red like the skin where the holy knife met,
Red like the blood trickling on the dirt,
Red like the unholy stain on my white skirt.

Red is the colour of my palms when I wed,
A colour as holy as the vermillion on my forehead.
And yet, conveniently swept under the carpet
In conversations over tea and bread.

Who will teach your daughter now?
That she deserves no lesser love.
No need for shame, it’s not a sin,
To be female in a world for men.

But these insidious whispers tell me otherwise.
A vagina’s shame covered up in age old lies.
They tell me I am impure, so take away my God,
Isolate me, punish me for my wrong.

The older women train me to be a lesser person,
To be happily married and happily forgotten.
They’ve learnt to drape their sin,
But it fits ill on my growing skin.

I beg time to teach me to unsee
The stolen glances on my sinful body.
Yet, the men keep watching, gawking, they see my agony.
Their guilt, too bitter, so they make the seen unseen.

My body, the only thing I can own, is worthless. Surely, I must be cursed.
I want to peel my skin and siphon out this worthless blood.
But forgive me dear brothers and sisters, then the murder will be yours.
This air you will have to share with this wretched sister of yours.
