A Rush of Blood to the Head
- Abhirami

Run to your bedroom, slam the door,
stick your head onto the bed, bending double.
The rush to the head will be intense,
nearly blinding. And just as comforting
as it had been nearly eleven years earlier
when Mindy left. Remember Mindy?
Daddy gave her to you the day
before he walked out (and much later,
you wished you could’ve asked him
if a kitten could compensate for a father.) White with black patches at odd places
that made you laugh out loud then,
when you were easier to amuse.
When she left (you still can’t think “died”
without a lump forming in your throat
that grows bigger, not smaller, with time)
It felt like someone had suddenly
replaced the air in your lungs
with water? It’s hard to tell.
But the rush helped, it always did.
For at least a few minutes, you forgot
the world and everything in it
(even that horrible, overwhelming pain)
and made you feel as though you were
floating in some safe limbo
where fathers never abandoned families
and beloved kittens never left –
even though Mumma always said,
“Too much blood to your brain
will make you a vegetable and die.”
So you stopped doing it, heaven knows
you didn’t want to die, not then,
you wanted to grow up and be big
and make Mumma smile again. But now
there’s no point to it, so run upstairs
to your room and stick your head
into your bed and do nothing but feel
the rush – and hope and pray you do
turn into a vegetable after all,
because the only person who ever cared
has left, and nothing else matters anymore.
