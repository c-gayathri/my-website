I wake up into a 24 hour void. A blank in my calendar. A rare blank. To fill as I please. I stick my tongue out to taste the day. A Saturday, yes. But what does it taste like? Like salty sweet cotton candy. Crisp. Disintegrates at the tip of my tongue. Disintegrates into a pink void.

My room is dark and stuffy. Not fluffy pink like fluffy cotton candy fluff. So I carry a laundry bag, detergent and a book. Up the stairs. Up more stairs. The terrace-top washing machine with a view of the cityscape gobbles up the laundry bag.

I climb up more stairs till there’s nothing more to climb. Except the roof of the room on the roof. Balanced precariously on railings that yawn halfway up to the top of the room on the roof, I hop on to it. The topmost top of the building. The building itself higher than any other building. Any building that was higher was too far away. I lie down on a recently painted patch of the roof of the room on the roof. It hotpresses yellow powder to my calves, my shorts, tee and hair. Worry is postponed. Worry will meet patting down and a hair-wash later.

In the meanwhile, a salty sweet wind is shared between yellow and pink matchbox buildings like a giggly secret. Then they return to their prim still lives. Salty sweet fluffs of cotton candy look down, not seeing, in a lazy afternoon stroll past a powder blue sky.

I drown in printed ink on yellow pages. Second-hand, nah, third-hand pages. An indigo IndiGo boarding pass sticks out like a bookmark. The cover, the same olive green my dad drowned in 10 years ago.

A city-sized population of fluffy cotton-candy fluffs lazily stroll by.

A momentary sound of footsteps. A visitor. A stranger. A pair of surprised eyes. Yet another pair of surprised eyebrows. A secret is shared from a pair of surprised eyes to a pair of surprised brows. And we get back to our prim stillness.

She takes her regular spot, her regular claim to rationed sanity between two water tanks. Lights a cigarette. Breathes out a gray puff. A fluffy puff like disintegrating cotton candy.

She asks me about my life. I ask her about hers. A salty sweet wind tells us we already know each other. We’d shared a secret, remember? A secret meant for people who watch cotton candy fluffs and breathe out cotton candy puffs.

She is 24 years old. Working. Attended a college nearby. Lived with her aunt. Born and brought up in Bangalore. Do your parents live far then? You didn’t stay with them? My mom passed away in 2013. My dad married again. Lives with another woman. I visit him. Don’t stay with him

Continuous, even words.

The stillness of a powder blue sky and matchbox yellows and pinks inherited by her brows.

I don’t offer an apology that can’t set history right, put gray puff back in cigarette stubs and unfurrow an 18 year old’s furrowed brows mangled in tears. Brows that will be forever unfurrowed after.

No apology was expected.

This contract was conveyed in the secret we shared.

In the salty sweet air.

In fluffy cotton candy. Crisp.

Under a powder blue sky.

Second day in a strange city. A city of white fluffs on a stroll. A matchbox spotted skyline. A fluff of thought. A girl with brows that didn’t move. Lips that puffed out gray puffs. A heart that once inhaled a gray puff and withdrew into a matchbox. A matchbox that could sit in a square hole. Squares to box away inconvenient things a red beating thing said. Humanoid carcasses bereaved of whispers from a red beating thing. Carcasses, slaving away, tick tock tick.  Gridlines to hold up carcasses. Like the lines on a calendar without blanks. Lines that map the second to the hour to the year. Like purple lines in a metro. Instructing, coaxing, cold. Straight angular purple lines like a pair of visitor’s eyebrows. Purple lines don’t move. Calculated unstupid whisperless purple lines.
