Have you ever just left a place knowing you will never return, having buried all those memories, jumped over its’ grave to even it out, resumed your life in an alternate universe, and then WHAM! A slap from the dead. You are now Scrooge traversing that past with fond bitter-sweet memories.

The prospect of my dad’s farewell party was not singularly exciting to me. We lived in a different part of the city, away from the Naval neighbourhood . So I did not expect many familiar faces. But, as we entered the mess through the bar, the familiarity hit me.

That fish tank nestled on its ledge. And I am back to 10 years ago. Twas the first clownfish I ever saw. I called it Nemo. It was at my friends birthday party and I remember my mom saying “Look at how neatly she has stacked up all her gifts. Will you ever learn to be this organised?” I must have grown an inch shorter that day.

The sprawling portico is a tesseract to yet another birthday party. Hordes of kids fighting for their return gifts, each one of em way more buoyant than me and me wondering if I will ever be one of those kids. With hindsight, I wouldn’t change anything. The return gift: a pretty little blue barbie diary with a heart shaped window and the tiniest lock. That was precious. Still is, probably tucked away somewhere among my things.

That little palm tree, or what is it, in the corner of the lawn, decked in fairy lights. We stood beneath that magic the first time I came here. My friend and I had previously argued over the existence of a certain garment: “the pantskirt”. She’d turned up in a mini skirt over leggings. No quantum of my arguments against it would suffice. She won. She was the bully after all.

What is it about memories that tugs you back no matter how they hurt? Like the lover you shouldn’t go back to. Maybe, because one day it had been my air, the atmosphere I breathed. And now it’s not.
