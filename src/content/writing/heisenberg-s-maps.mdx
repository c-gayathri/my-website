There is a snake living in my back. It coils around. Courses through my back. Hides in wonky spots behind bones I can’t reach. It needs constant love. Daily. Without fail. It throws a tantrum otherwise. When I stroke it, it melts away into temporary nothingness, to come back again tomorrow. When I play with it (it demands movement), it dissolves away for the night. Sometimes it bites back. Scintillating pinpricks of pain. The kind that evades touch. Evades locus. Only a general sense of being there. Heisenberg’s mathematics on my back. The doctor tells me I am imagining it. Sometimes I think I do. Other times it is as real as the stiff bulge of a muscle, the vibrating twitch of a fibre, the cracks and buckling in a joint.

It’s like the sky, I tell my lover. It’s constant and yet never the same every day. Romanticising my daily negotiation with my body? Perhaps.

Or like Mumbai roads during the monsoon, he says back. Every day, the potholes look different. Now that’s an intimate relationship, I giggle. You gotta know your Mumbai roads and how to navigate them like the skin and body of a lover. The sun dances differently on it each day, your touch on his skin feels different each day. You reach out to feel the difference, to make sense of it. Perhaps that way you can hold on to it, control it. The difference evades, lies just under the translucence of his skin (like ripples of light at the bottom of a shallow pool), but dances away at your touch. It keeps it alive, you say. Keeps you guessing.

The love, older now, curdles the air between us, a different sourness each day. The inflection of his voice is the same, but I could swear it was different from the sweetness of our first date. His touch recalls an old irritation, renewed and more poignant, nearly the same as yesterday, but with new margins. It’s the same face, but it’s not. I pick a new fight every day about the same old thing. Keeps him guessing, I say.

The love becomes a recurring fever dream, one you know is supposed to be sweet and lovely but jumbles up all the words and gets it wrong. So you will yourself back to sleep, back into your lover’s arms, to fix it; you see the same godforsaken dream again but its outlines only shift ever so slightly, the force of its prearranged destiny writes out every scene; the shape of your irritation ripples and settles back into the stubborn familiarity of the face you wake up to every day.

The pain is my lover. A new face every day. It embraces me in coily fingers. Burnishes its terror into a new sinew. Sinews I didn’t know existed. They throw tantrums, cry for my attention. Lover’s curse, I say. The pain radiates down my limbs, down my back, up my scalp. A new pain map each day. The same map traced with different hands. Like a pile of one-rupee political maps of India, rivers drawn for a geography class.

Show me where, he had said, as he traced his hands down Ganges, and up Godavari. He can read pain, I remember thinking.

Now, he writes it. His touch as vile as the snake.

The rivers shift. Water boring into the soft skin of the earth. The potholes crack under the gush. They are filled up with cement only to be flushed out again. I visit all the doctors, do my daily exercises, but the rivers keep boring in.

Every day I wake up and note the the new and old rhythm of my lover’s breathing (was it always so loud?); and the shapelessness of the snake in my back, the pinpricks that dance in formation (a ritual offering for Heisenberg), and the inaccessible angles of my shoulders and hip. I look for a clue, for what today holds, for an end to the repeating dreams.

Because, if the lover breathes wrong, we’ll relive the new shapes of our old hate; and if the snake demands it, I offer my day up to it, stroking and melting it away in ritualistic devotion. The morning gods smile down, they choose the shape of the day. Too lazy, pick a one-rupee political map from the pile of geography submissions. Yawn. Head out to their game of golf.
